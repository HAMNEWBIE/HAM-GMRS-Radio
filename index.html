GMRS

462 / 467 MHz

The General Mobile Radio Service shares its 22 channels with FRS. A GMRS license costs $35, lasts 10 years, requires no exam, and covers your whole immediate family. FRS use of the same channels needs no license at lower power.

Repeater use: transmit on the 467 MHz input, listen on the 462 MHz output, with whatever tone the repeater owner publishes. Find repeaters and request access on myGMRS.

Project LongHaul: a nationwide push to connect GMRS operators across the USA. Program 462.675 (channel 20) with a 141.3 tone on transmit and receive, then call out at 8 or 11, morning or evening Eastern, plus 2 PM and 2 AM, and see who answers. That's the channel on the B line of the radio up top.

Mesh networks

902–928 MHz ISM / 2.4 + 5.8 GHz

License-free encrypted texting over LoRa on one side, licensed high-bandwidth IP networking on the other. On the LoRa side this site runs MeshCore.

LORA MeshCore

Off-grid, end-to-end encrypted text and position sharing over LoRa, in the 902–928 MHz ISM band in the US, so no license is required. Runs on cheap hardware (Heltec V3, RAK, LILYGO T-Deck and 50+ other boards). Nodes only route through designated repeaters instead of every radio re-flooding every packet, which keeps busy meshes usable, and room servers give you shared group channels. Range is line-of-sight: one repeater on a hill covers everyone below.

meshcore.io

LORA Meshtastic

The other big LoRa mesh, same band and mostly the same hardware, with a larger installed base but flood-based routing. Worth knowing about since a board flashed for one can be reflashed for the other in minutes.

meshtastic.org

AREDN Amateur mesh

Ham-licensed data networking on modified WiFi hardware in the 2.4 and 5.8 GHz amateur allocations. Real IP bandwidth for cameras, VoIP, and services between nodes. Requires at least a Technician license, and Part 97 traffic cannot be encrypted.

arednmesh.org
